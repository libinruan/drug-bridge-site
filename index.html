Why now

Atopic dermatitis is driven by a well-understood inflammatory signal — one that current biologic and oral therapies each address with real trade-offs, between delivery burden and broader safety exposure. QRK-005/007 are designed to intervene precisely at that signal's convergence point, as a pill.

This is not an unproven idea: the broader field of oral STAT6-targeted therapies has drawn major pharma investment and encouraging early clinical signals over the past two years. DrugBridge is entering a category the market has already validated, with a differentiated pair of candidates and a clear path to IND.

The Science

One target, engineered for precision

Two signaling proteins, IL-4 and IL-13, drive the inflammation behind atopic dermatitis. Both converge on a single downstream switch: STAT6. QRK-005 and QRK-007 are designed to shut that switch off directly.

Targeted at the source

By acting on STAT6 itself, one molecule addresses what upstream biologic and kinase-inhibitor approaches currently split between two different drug classes.

Built for selectivity

Both candidates are engineered to act on STAT6 with high specificity, sparing related signaling proteins — the design principle behind a cleaner safety profile than broad-spectrum alternatives.

Oral by design

A small molecule taken as a pill, aimed at delivering biologic-level efficacy without an injection.

A validated approach

Oral STAT6-targeted therapy is an active, closely watched category: multiple programs are in clinical and preclinical development, several already backed by major pharmaceutical partnerships. Public clinical signals from the most advanced program in the class — degradation of the target protein alongside meaningful improvement in disease severity — support the underlying biology DrugBridge's candidates are built on.

Pipeline & Landscape

A crowded field, an open lead

Approved biologics and oral therapies for atopic dermatitis each leave something on the table — delivery convenience, or breadth of safety exposure. A wave of oral STAT6 programs, DrugBridge's included, is racing to close that gap.

Injectable biologics
Established efficacy and safety, but require regular injection — a real burden for chronic daily use.
Oral JAK inhibitors
Fast-acting and convenient, but broad-spectrum action carries a boxed safety warning.
Oral STAT6 degraders
The category DrugBridge is building in — designed for injectable-level efficacy with the convenience and, potentially, the safety profile of a targeted pill.

A field pharma is already betting on

Six competing oral STAT6 programs are in development worldwide. Four are already partnered with large pharmaceutical companies — most secured before any Phase 2 data existed, proof the category has real buyer demand. The most clinically advanced program remains independently owned by its developer, and has reported encouraging Phase 1 results while now enrolling Phase 2. DrugBridge's QRK-005/007 are unpartnered and pre-IND, competing on data quality and deal economics in a category the market has already validated.
